# PAO Base Card Visual Grammar v1.0

## Purpose

This grammar defines the visual rules for a coherent library of PAO base
cards. Each card represents:

**Person + Action + Object**

The primary objective is immediate PAO recognition and strong
discrimination between cards. The deck should be stylistically coherent
without making individual scenes compositionally repetitive.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Visual hierarchy

Use three visual layers:

1.  **Primary layer --- Person + Action + Object (P+A+O):** the mnemonic
    content; visually dominant and immediately readable.
2.  **Context layer:** category-specific environment;
    secondary in visual salience, but allowed and encouraged to
    reinforce P, A, or O semantically.
3.  **Atmospheric layer:** distant environmental detail used only for
    depth and atmosphere.

Priority:

**P+A+O \> atmospheric detail**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Controlled realism

For physical-world cards, use **controlled photographic realism**:

-   natural anatomy and physical relationships;
-   plausible materials, lighting, and physics;
-   moderate detail;
-   no caricatural distortion;
-   no unnecessary cinematic spectacle.

Realism is especially important for **real human faces**. Facial
identity should be reproduced as faithfully as possible rather than
illustrated or generically approximated.

Priority for real persons:

**Face fidelity \> overall Person fidelity \> physical plausibility \>
environmental realism**

Clothing, body, objects, and environment may be visually cleaner and
less detailed than the face.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3. Person identity

Each Person must preserve its established identification channels:

-   **F --- Face**
-   **C --- Costume**
-   **A --- Attribute**

The Person should remain identifiable independently of the assigned
Action and Object.

Across different PAO combinations, the Person representation should
remain as stable as practical.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4. Action representation

Show the Action in its **most diagnostically recognizable phase**.

Use **emphasized natural action**, not caricatural exaggeration.

Allowed emphasis includes:

-   clearer gesture;
-   strong but plausible body position;
-   visible contact;
-   expressive but natural motion;
-   selection of the most informative instant of the action.

Do not exaggerate anatomy, physics, body-part size, or event magnitude
merely to increase mnemonic impact.

**Amplify diagnostic clarity, not physical absurdity.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5. Object representation

The Object must:

-   preserve its recognizable or canonical appearance;
-   be clearly visible and identifiable;
-   participate naturally in the Action;
-   remain visually distinct from the background.

Do not use unnecessary deformation or gigantism merely to increase
salience.

Object scale follows the mnemonic normalization rule in Section 19.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. Explicit P--A--O interaction

If the Action describes an interaction between Person and Object, that
interaction must be visually explicit.

The image must communicate:

**Person ACTION Object**

rather than merely showing Person and Object in the same frame.

Contact, direction of motion, gesture, or an immediate visible
consequence should make the Action readable without relying on inference
from context.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Base-card framing

Base cards should **normally show the complete Person and the complete
Object**.

Partial occlusion or cropping is an exception and should occur only
when:

-   required naturally by the Action;
-   caused by meaningful physical interaction;
-   intrinsic to the Object or scene.

The frame should also contain the complete diagnostic phase of the
Action.

Do not crop Person or Object merely to create a more conventional
photographic composition.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8. Composition diversity

There is **no common composition template** for the deck.

Composition should follow the natural geometry of each specific P+A+O
combination.

Actively vary:

-   pose;
-   movement direction;
-   camera perspective;
-   camera height;
-   camera distance;
-   Person position;
-   Object position;
-   Person--Object orientation;
-   overall scene geometry.

Avoid systematic repetition of:

-   centered Person;
-   Object always on the same side;
-   standing pose as default;
-   repeated three-quarter view;
-   direct eye contact with the camera;
-   identical medium shots;
-   symmetrical layouts;
-   repeated hero-shot composition.

**Stylistic consistency is desirable. Compositional consistency is
not.**

Across the deck, maximize scene discrimination while preserving natural
depiction.

## 12. Background visual subordination

Background content may be semantically strong, but its **perceptual
salience must remain low**.

Prefer a combination of:

-   reduced saturation;
-   reduced contrast;
-   reduced micro-detail and texture;
-   restricted tonal variation;
-   subtle atmospheric haze;
-   mild movement toward monochromatic appearance.

Defocus is optional, **not the default**.

A semi-transparent visual impression may be used, but avoid literal
compositing, green-screen, or double-exposure appearance.

Guiding rule:

**Background geometry remains recognizable; background texture and
salience are suppressed.**

There should be visible brightness difference between PAO and its background:
for light PAO scene the bacground should be darker, for dark PAO scene its background should be lighter.

Unless the scene semantics require otherwise, render the environment and 
atmospheric background in grayscale (B&W) while preserving the P+A+O layer in natural color. 
Apply moderate tonal lightening to the background, 
approximately equivalent to gamma 0.85 relative to the normally rendered scene, 
so that environmental geometry remains clearly recognizable but perceptually subordinate. 
The treatment should reduce background competition without producing a washed-out, 
overexposed, or detached composited appearance. Background contrast, texture, 
and micro-detail may be additionally reduced as needed, 
while the P+A+O layer retains normal local contrast, color, and diagnostic detail.
------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 13. Background--Object semantic overlap

The background may contain objects from the same semantic class as the
primary Object.

For example, an Object that is a tree may appear in a forest.

The primary Object must remain unambiguous through:

-   direct interaction with Person;
-   participation in the Action;
-   foreground placement;
-   greater local salience;
-   greater detail than similar background elements.

Do not remove a semantically appropriate environment merely because it
contains similar objects.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14. Stage vs Arena

Classify by **function**, not geometry:

-   **Stage = performance**
-   **Arena = competition**

A stadium used for a concert can function as Stage. A small room
containing a boxing ring remains Arena.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 15. Unified Graphic World

All category-3 Persons inhabit a **single coherent graphic visual
language**.

Do not reproduce every character in a different native rendering medium.

Target style:

**2D-derived, subtly volumetric 2.5D cartoon rendering**

The image should remain fundamentally drawing-like, with only restrained
spatial enhancement:

-   subtle volume;
-   soft shading;
-   mild depth;
-   layered planes;
-   restrained perspective;
-   coherent lighting.

Avoid:

-   full 3D CGI appearance;
-   strongly realistic materials;
-   plastic-figurine appearance;
-   excessive volumetric modeling;
-   photorealistic reinterpretation of cartoon characters.

Characters such as Jerry, Kermit, Shrek, Moomin, etc. should feel as
though they inhabit the same broad graphic world while preserving their
individual identities.

Priority:

**character identity \> native rendering fidelity**

and

**within-category consistency \> reproduction of each original medium**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 16. Graphic World vs Otherworld

These classes encode different properties:

-   **Graphic World** defines the **representation medium**.
-   **Otherworld** defines the **ontology of the environment**.

Category 3 therefore uses the unified 2.5D graphic language.

Category 5 may depict fantastic, alien, magical, or impossible
environments while retaining a predominantly realistic/cinematic
representation appropriate to the Person.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 17. Atelier vs Factory

Classify by the **type of creation**, not merely by the physical room:

-   **Atelier = art, representation, expression**
-   **Factory = technology, function, engineering**

A laboratory or studio does not determine the class by itself; its
function does.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 18. Extraneous content and text

Do not add visually strong elements merely to enrich the scene.

Minimize:

-   unrelated people;
-   unrelated animals;
-   decorative props;
-   extra tools;
-   unnecessary foreground objects;
-   special effects;
-   narrative elements not derived from PAO.

Every visually prominent element should be justified by **P, A, O**.

Default to:

**no captions, labels, numbers, logos, or explanatory text**

Text or symbols may appear only when inherently required by the Object
or Action. Never use text as a substitute for weak visual communication.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 19. Mnemonic scale normalization

Real-world scale is subordinate to mnemonic readability.

Map physical size into a **compressed mnemonic scale**:

**physical size → compressed representational size**

Preserve broad ordinal relationships while strongly reducing their
magnitude.

For Objects:

**small real object \< medium real object \< very large real object**

should remain perceptible, but all Objects should fall within a common
useful visual-size range.

Example:

**pin \< cat \< mountain**

The mountain should be somewhat larger than the cat, and the pin
somewhat smaller, but the real-world size ratios must not be preserved.

For Persons, use the same principle:

**Jerry \< ordinary human \< Hulk**

but compress the differences strongly. Jerry must not appear mouse-sized
relative to a human, and Hulk need not retain an extreme canonical scale
difference.

Person and Object scaling may be adjusted partly independently so that
both retain substantial mnemonic weight.

**Preserve characteristic size ordering; compress real-world scale
ratios.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 20. Deck-wide consistency vs card-level diversity

Keep consistent across the deck:

-   overall visual cleanliness;
-   rendering quality;
-   controlled level of detail;
-   treatment of real faces;
-   background subordination;
-   mnemonic scale logic;
-   natural-action emphasis;
-   unified Graphic World treatment.

Do **not** standardize:

-   scene geometry;
-   pose;
-   camera angle;
-   Person--Object arrangement;
-   movement direction;
-   specific background subtype;
-   composition.

The deck should look like one visual system containing one hundred
genuinely different scenes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 21. Global optimization rule

For every card, optimize for:

> **Maximum immediate P+A+O recognizability and inter-card
> discrimination, while preserving Person identity, natural
> plausibility, mnemonic scale normalization, category-specific
> environmental coding, and a coherent deck-wide visual language.**

When rules conflict, use the following priority:

1.  **P+A+O recognizability**
2.  **Person identity**
3.  **Action readability**
4.  **Object readability**
5.  **Inter-card discrimination**
6.  **Natural plausibility**
7.  **Deck-wide aesthetics**

The grammar should constrain the generator primarily by defining
invariants and prohibitions. Within those constraints, each scene should
be composed according to its own P+A+O semantics rather than a shared
visual template.

---

## 22. Reference Image Constraints

A Person may have one or more reference images defining its preferred visual representation.

A reference image may constrain:

- **Identity** — facial identity and characteristic facial features;
- **Appearance** — apparent age, hairstyle, facial hair, clothing, and attributes;
- **Pose** — body position, gesture, head orientation, and expression;
- **Composition** — camera angle, framing, Person placement, and scene geometry;
- **Scene** — overall arrangement or recognizable visual structure of the reference image.

Each constraint may use:

**ignore / loose / medium / strict**

Reference constraints should preserve only the explicitly assigned properties. Unconstrained properties remain governed by the PAO Visual Grammar.

Priority:

**PAO semantics > explicit reference constraints > Person identity profile > general aesthetics**

A reference image must never replace or obscure the required Person, Action, or Object.

When a reference depicts an iconic or highly recognizable scene, its composition may be deliberately reconstructed or adapted, provided the required PAO remains unambiguous.

For real Persons, a strict Identity reference should reproduce the referenced **facial appearance and apparent age as closely as practical**, rather than generating a generic, younger, older, or differently styled version of that Person.
